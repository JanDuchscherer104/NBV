# Reader-centred exposition

Read this for multi-paragraph, section, chapter, outline, or prose-flow work.
Optimize for a technically competent reader who does not already possess the
author's project context.

## Intellectual spine

State the central problem, what has been established, what remains unresolved,
and why the next unit is necessary. Order material by epistemic dependency,
not research chronology, repository layout, or source order. Ground a concept
before later reasoning depends on it.

For each substantial unit, record the transition:

`enters knowing X -> resolves Y -> leaves knowing Z -> Z enables the next unit`

If deleting the unit loses no necessary understanding, evidence, motivation,
qualification, or connection, compress, relocate, or remove it.

## Paragraph and sentence flow

Give each paragraph one dominant rhetorical move: motivate, define, compare,
derive, demonstrate, qualify, or connect. Use context-content-conclusion as a
default reasoning shape:

1. orient from established context or the active reader question;
2. advance evidence, explanation, or analysis;
3. land the interpretation or consequence the next paragraph can inherit.

At sentence level, keep known information in topic position and place the
important new information toward stress position. Preserve stable terminology
and an intelligible thematic chain. Repair coherence by changing the reasoning
order before adding connective phrases; cohesion cannot rescue reversed or
missing dependencies.

## Synthesis and economy

Use synthesis bridges to state what a section established and why it enables
what follows. Prefer mechanisms, assumptions, evidence, limitations, and
consequences over generic academic phrasing. Scientific narrative exposes the
central logic early; it does not use suspense or reproduce the chronology of
the work.

Keep the main text self-contained for the central argument:

- **Dependency:** summarize every premise on which a central conclusion relies.
- **Comprehension:** retain what the reader needs to understand the method or
  result.
- **Validity:** retain what a skeptical examiner needs to assess a core claim.
- **Narrative load:** keep necessary semantics in the main text and move
  exhaustive realization detail to an appendix.
- **Repeat reference:** if ordinary reasoning repeatedly requires an appendix,
  repair the abstraction boundary.

Appendices may deepen, reproduce, or audit the argument; they do not own a
missing premise. Prefer high takeaway density: each subsection should change
what the reader can understand, assess, or use next.

## Completion and review lens

The candidate is structurally ready when every substantial unit has an incoming
reader state, one to three durable takeaways, and an outgoing dependency; every
paragraph lands a reader-facing consequence; concepts precede their uses; and
main-text placement passes the applicable tests above.

For a frozen-candidate review, report ungrounded concepts, reversed
dependencies, paragraphs without a landing consequence, generic transitions
masking incoherent order, or critical premises hidden only in an appendix.
Return findings without rewriting the candidate.

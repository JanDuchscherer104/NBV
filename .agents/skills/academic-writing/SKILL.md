# Academic Writing

Own argument construction: turn evidence from exact repository owners into
bounded, traceable academic content. Establish conceptual tension,
contribution, scope, limitation, and evidence before handing accepted content
to `typst-authoring`. Do not edit Typst realization or certify validity.

## Workflow

1. Read the nearest docs guide and open the exact literature, bibliography,
   code, experiment, and active-document owners needed for the argument. Treat
   those sources as authoritative; do not make a skill-owned evidence store.
2. For a section or paragraph, read
   [`section-contract.md`](./assets/templates/section-contract.md), then use
   [`source-grounded-workflow.md`](references/source-grounded-workflow.md).
   For thesis/proposal prose or section acceptance, also read
   [`thesis-writing.md`](references/thesis-writing.md) and
   [`thesis-section-contracts.md`](references/thesis-section-contracts.md).
   Use [`upstream-matt-writing.md`](references/upstream-matt-writing.md) only
   when scratch fragment, shape, or beat mechanics are applicable.
3. For Related Work or concept-centered synthesis, also read
   [`literature-synthesis.md`](references/literature-synthesis.md).
4. Propagate only bounded claims with explicit evidence and limitations.
   Unsupported strength becomes a limitation or hypothesis.
5. Handoff by naming the destination and bounded payload: accepted content plus
   its contract for `typst-authoring`, or the unresolved validity concern for
   `scientific-review`. Keep the destination skill unloaded until executing its
   work.

## Completion

The argument is ready when scope, evidence, citation identity, limitation,
and destination are explicit and no exact source has been copied into the
skill layer.
